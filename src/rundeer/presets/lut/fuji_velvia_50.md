# Fujifilm Velvia 50 — LUT Reference

## Overview

Fujifilm Velvia 50 (RVP 50) is an ISO 50 daylight-balanced (5500K) reversal (slide) film, legendary among landscape and nature photographers for its hyper-saturated, jewel-like color rendering. Its extremely fine grain, ultra-high sharpness, and deliberate oversaturation of certain hue ranges make it the most visually distinctive film stock ever made. The look is not "realistic" — it is heightened, almost surreal, yet unmistakably photographic.

---

## Tone Curve Characteristics

### Shadows (0–20% luminance)
- **Toe shape:** Short, abrupt toe — shadows crush quickly to deep inky black. Very little shadow lift.
- **Minimum density:** True black with virtually no base lift. Blacks are dense and opaque.
- **Shadow color:** Neutral to very slightly cool. Deep shadows in foliage areas pick up a dark cyan-green.
- **Detail retention:** Limited shadow latitude — one of Velvia's known weaknesses. Expose to the right.

### Midtones (20–70% luminance)
- **Gamma:** Approximately 0.75 — slightly steeper than negative film, giving punchy contrast.
- **Skin tones:** Rendered with a warm-orange cast. Flattering for lighter skin tones; can push deeper skin tones slightly too red.
- **Color saturation:** Aggressively boosted across the board, especially greens, reds, and yellows.
- **Foliage greens:** Pushed toward vivid yellow-green. A defining characteristic of the stock.

### Highlights (70–100% luminance)
- **Shoulder shape:** Short shoulder — highlights compress and clip relatively quickly.
- **White point:** Very bright. Whites are clean with no warmth — reversal stock doesn't have the orange mask.
- **Sky rendering:** Blues in highlights take on a deep, rich cobalt quality with a slight cyan shift.

## Grain Characteristics

- **Type:** Extremely fine, uniform grain — one of the finest-grained film stocks ever made.
- **Grain size:** Almost imperceptible at 6×6cm or larger scan. At 35mm, very fine but visible.
- **Color grain:** Tri-layer coupler separation occasionally creates faint magenta chroma grain in shadow areas.
- **Suggested digital equivalent:** Luminance noise σ=0.004, very small grain kernel (radius 0.5px).

---

## Use Cases

- Landscape photography: the primary intended use. Skies, forests, mountains.
- Product and still life with vibrant color objects.
- Animation backgrounds and environment plates that need heightened, painterly color.
- Any scene where the color should feel more vivid than reality.

---

## Pairing Notes

- **Avoid for:** Skin-forward close-ups (the orange push becomes unflattering). Low-light scenes (shadow latitude is too limited).
- **Works well with:** Wide landscape shots, nature documentaries, fantasy world-building.
- **Complementary grade:** Keep the base grade neutral — Velvia already does the heavy lifting.
- **Reference use:** Nature documentary titles, landscape fine art printing, travel photography, anime background matte paintings.
