1. **Title:** Jean 'Moebius' Giraud - Unseen Dune | Jodorowsky on Jean "Moebius" Giraud  
   **URL:** https://www.duneinfo.com/unseen/moebius  
   **Source/Context:** Dedicated page on Jodorowsky's unproduced Dune film pre-production (circa 1975). Features extended first-person account by Alejandro Jodorowsky describing recruiting and collaborating with Jean Giraud (Moebius) as storyboard artist/concept designer. Primary attributed source (likely translated from French interview or Jodorowsky's writings on the project). Highly detailed on technique and visual output. Includes examples of the resulting drawings (costumes, characters like Baron Harkonnen, Paul Atreides, etc.).  
   **Verbatim Passage(s) / Quoted Text (full relevant section):**  
   "I needed a precise script... I wanted to carry out film on paper before filming it... These days all films with special effect are done as that, but at the time this technique was not used. I wanted a draughtsman of comic strips who has the genius and the speed, who can be used as me as a camera and who gives at the same time a visual style... I was by chance with my second warrior: Jean Giraud alias Moebius (at the time he had not made Arzach, nor The Airtight Garage). I say to him: 'If you accept this work, you must all give up and leave tomorrow with me to Los Angeles to speak with Douglas Trumbull (2001: A Space Odyssey)'. Moebius asked for a few hours of think about it. The following day, we left for the United States. ... Giraud made 3000 drawings, all marvellous... The script of Dune, thanks to his talent, is a masterpiece. One can see living the characters, one follows the movements of camera. One visualizes cutting, the decorations, the costumes... All that with, each time, some features of pencil... I was behind his shoulders by asking him for the various points of view... By putting in scene the 'actors', etc One filmed..."  
   **Full Context Notes & Prompt Engineering Value:** Jodorowsky sought a comic artist for pre-visualization ("film on paper") to create a "precise script" with cinematic qualities. Emphasizes Moebius's speed, genius, and ability to simultaneously deliver "visual style" while functioning "as a camera." Result: 3000 drawings capturing "living the characters," camera movements, editing ("cutting"), detailed sets ("decorations"), costumes. Technique: "some features of pencil" (suggests economical, expressive, clean yet evocative linework); Jodorowsky directing POVs and staging from behind the artist. Imaginative worlds: Blends Herbert's universe with Jodorowsky's psychedelic/spiritual vision through Moebius's detailed, organic-futuristic designs (e.g., anti-grav, grotesque characters like obese Baron). Ideal for prompts: "cinematic storyboard style, precise pencil linework with dynamic camera angles and points of view, living expressive characters, intricate detailed costumes and organic futuristic architecture, 1970s Moebius Dune concept art aesthetic, surreal yet tangible sci-fi world-building."

2. **Title:** Jodorowsky's Dune Movie Script (or related transcript/source)  
   **URL:** https://www.scripts.com/script-pdf-body.php?id=11338 (search snippet context; likely hosts PDF or body of Jodorowsky Dune script with production notes/transcript)  
   **Source/Context:** Appears to contain or quote Jodorowsky recounting the recruitment and working method with Moebius during Dune pre-production. Complements the duneinfo.com account.  
   **Verbatim Passage(s) / Quoted Text (from search result snippet):**  
   "He say, 'I am Moebius.' 'When I drawing science fiction, I am not Giraud, I am Moebius.' And I say to him, 'You are the person! Come with me!' Drawing by drawing, I shoot the picture. I need 3000 drawings. Point of view. Movement of the camera. Dialogue. The relation between the actors. I use Moebius like a camera. I take Moebius, I say, 'Now...'"  
   **Full Context Notes & Prompt Engineering Value:** Reinforces the "use Moebius like a camera" metaphor. Highlights Moebius's alter-ego/persona shift for sci-fi work ("When I drawing science fiction, I am not Giraud, I am Moebius"). Process: Sequential "drawing by drawing" to "shoot the picture" with explicit cinematic elements (POV, camera movement, dialogue, actor relations). Supports prompts emphasizing cinematic sequencing, persona-driven surreal sci-fi linework, fluid progression of imaginative scenes.

3. **Title:** The Incal - Jodoverse Wiki / Collaboration Process Description  
   **URL:** https://jodoverse.fandom.com/wiki/The_Incal  
   **Source/Context:** Fandom wiki dedicated to Jodorowsky's universe (The Incal saga). Details the writing/drawing process for the seminal 1980-1988 series with Moebius. Draws from documented accounts of their collaboration (likely including Jodorowsky interviews or statements in editions/intros). Also covers universe influences (mysticism, PK Dick, alchemy) and visual synergy.  
   **Verbatim Passage(s) / Quoted Text or Close Attribution:**  
   "The writing process consisted of Jodorowsky dictating the story while Moebius took drawing notes and occasionally suggested dialogues. ... Moebius had a particular schedule: he did one page per day. It was stressful but it also permitted him to work more freely and intuitively." (Process description attributed to the Jodorowsky-Moebius collaboration on The Incal.)  
   **Full Context Notes & Prompt Engineering Value:** Jodorowsky's story dictation paired with Moebius's real-time visual interpretation/notes and dialogue suggestions = highly collaborative, improvisational creation of surreal, metaphysical worlds. Daily one-page pace enabled "freely and intuitively" drawing — suggests fluid, organic linework evolving naturally rather than over-planned. Imaginative worlds: "Jodoverse" blending space opera, mysticism, satire, grotesque/surreal elements (e.g., The Black/Luminous Incal duality, androgynous figures, dream-like sequences). For prompts: "Jodorowsky-Moebius Incal style, intuitive daily comic page process, fluid organic linework with surreal metaphysical elements, detailed yet dynamic character expressions and bizarre organic architecture/futuristic tech, philosophical sci-fi comic aesthetic, one-page self-contained yet epic narrative composition."
